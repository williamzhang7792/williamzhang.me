The puzzle in one paragraph:

When a foundation model agent acts on someone's behalf and causes harm, the chain of liability is genuinely novel. The data is sparse, the failure modes are correlated in ways that classical actuarial methods don't model well, and the few existing markets (Armilla, AIUC) are still bootstrapping their pricing assumptions.

There's a lot of writing about *whether* AI risk is insurable. Less about how you'd actually price it. That's the gap I want to write into.
